# User-painted Skarmory: HP IV deduction and controlled-change study

## Preserved checkpoint

`fixtures/solver-example-skarmory.json` is the unmodified user export of `curtain-observations (5).json`. `fixtures/skarmory-test.ek3` is the matching 80-byte encrypted reference file. The JSON, not a rendering of the reference file, supplies the regression observations. It includes 160 observed main-record nibbles, 32 unknown neighboring nibbles, level 16 summary stats (51/35/59/32/19/32), known trainer IDs/species/nature/ability/gender, zero EVs/condition and explicit defaults, EXP 5120, and an applied HP IV 31. No PID or other IV was supplied as a fact.

The user describes the Pokémon as a legal, nearly perfect Skarmory generated by their Gen 3 builder. The tool verifies record size, checksum and consistency, not legality or generation provenance. The reference has IVs 31/30/31/31/28/30. These reference values are test oracles, not facts borrowed by the solver.

- All 160 entered pixel observations match the reference; zero pixel or supplied-fact contradictions.
- Undoing only the supplied HP-IV fact in the saved JSON gives **41 PIDs**. Applying HP IV 31 narrows this to **20 PIDs**.
- HP stats alone give **27–31**; the curtain projection gives **7, 15, 23, 31**. Their intersection is **31**.
- Applying HP IV 31 leaves **20 PIDs**, all at block order 14, and retains the reference PID and every actual record byte.

## Why Rare Candies can help

At the pinned pret commit `731ad5bfd6e6f265508d0efcca0ba42f9dcf5881`, [`pokemon.c`](https://github.com/pret/pokeemerald/blob/731ad5bfd6e6f265508d0efcca0ba42f9dcf5881/src/pokemon.c) implements `ITEM3_LEVEL_UP` by setting EXP to the next level’s growth-table threshold and recalculating stats. [`item_effects.h`](https://github.com/pret/pokeemerald/blob/731ad5bfd6e6f265508d0efcca0ba42f9dcf5881/src/data/pokemon/item_effects.h) also applies friendship changes. Rare Candy does not change PID, original trainer IDs, IVs or EVs. Moves and PP may change if moves are learned. Friendship changes depend on its current range and bonuses; do not assume EXP is the only changed byte.

Skarmory uses Slow growth: next-level thresholds here are floor(5 × level³ / 4). A known plaintext EXP nibble `P` encrypts to `P XOR K`. An observed transparent pixel fixes `K = P`; black excludes `K = P`. Different known EXP values can therefore exclude additional keys, but many may yield the same black/transparent pattern. Higher-level summary readings can separately narrow shared IV ranges.

## Conditional forecast, not new in-game evidence

`scripts/probe-skarmory-candies.ts` uses the encrypted reference to simulate future EXP pixels and summary stats. It changes EXP and repairs the checksum in a copy; it never modifies the supplied file, live painting or emulator. It does not simulate friendship/move/PP side effects. All 20 starting candidates have the same block order, and those side-effect fields are unconstrained by this checkpoint’s applied model, so the displayed forecast isolates the EXP contribution. Actual follow-up captures are still needed to verify it.

| Checkpoint | EXP | PIDs shared with the initial capture and preceding checkpoints |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Saved level 16 | 5120 | 20 |
| Level 17, one Rare Candy | 6141 | 20 |
| Level 18, two Rare Candies | 7290 | 17 |

The predicted level-17 summary is 54/37/62/34/20/33. Intersecting level-16 and level-17 stat ranges establishes Speed IV 31; that additional IV does not itself reduce these 20 PIDs in the current model.

More simulated levels can help: preserving every intermediate EXP observation through level 25 leaves six of the original 20; accumulating summary-stat ranges establishes all six IVs by then and leaves four in this model. This is an experiment-design forecast, not evidence that those in-game measurements have occurred or a promise of unique recovery. The script also partitions the baseline PID list by predicted EXP patterns to identify potentially informative single checkpoints without using the true PID to choose the partition.

Running `npx tsx scripts/probe-skarmory-candies.ts 100` extends the straight-line forecast. With every intermediate checkpoint retained and all stat-derived exact IVs applied, it leaves two at level 35 and first leaves one at level 95 (79 Candies). This demonstrates unique recovery within this simulated model for this reference, not an efficient recommendation or an emulator-verified outcome. EXP-only evidence still leaves two at level 100. Better experiments should target differences between the remaining candidates instead of assuming each extra level gives useful information.

## Experiment protocol and current UI limit

Keep the saved level-16 JSON. Make an emulator backup, use one Rare Candy without battles, record exact level/EXP/max HP/all five other stats, return Skarmory to Box 8 slot 5, re-enter the school and save the new drawing under a distinct filename. Repeat once for level 18. Keep the same species and original trainer IDs; preserve the known EVs and previously established IVs. Treat any friendship input as potentially changed. Decline optional new moves for a simpler experiment.

The current app analyzes one checkpoint at a time. Replacing the first painting discards its EXP evidence: predicted standalone counts are 233 at level 17 and 199 at level 18 with the saved HP-IV fact. This is not a contradiction of the joint 20/17 forecast. Cross-checkpoint analysis must keep separate observations and EXP values and combine shared PID/OT ID/IV constraints. Do not merge changed ciphertext pixels into one static record mask. A future multi-observation mode should do this explicitly and retain old summary readings.

The original imported candidate file represents level 16. It is expected to contradict a later level’s EXP and pixels; clear it or supply an updated export for candidate comparison. Do not use the old candidate as current truth.

`tests/skarmory.test.ts` preserves the measured deduction and independent EXP-pattern forecast. No ACE, memory writes or modifications to the Pokémon are involved in this research task.
